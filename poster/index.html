SomaiyaSat Online: Inbound Content Marketing for a PocketQube Mission Website

1 Introduction

  • SomaiyaSat is a 5 cm PocketQube released by SomaiyaPod (use case KJS-SRS-01, with ReOrbit, Finland). After deployment it has one radio, an average power budget under 1 W, and only a short pass over a ground station in low Earth orbit.
  • Four downlink modes share that radio: TT&C housekeeping, Codec2 voice, M17 voice and data, and SSTV images. Something onboard has to decide, every second, which frame is both important and actually receivable.
  • The audiences — KJSIT and KJSSE students, faculty, the collaborator, and the global amateur-radio (HAM) community — are not in one room. The motivation for this study is to give that mission a public digital channel.
  • The real-world product is already deployed at somaiyasat.vercel.app: a mission site plus a ground station whose routing line is computed on the server, not played back as a scripted animation.

2 Problem statement

  • A student space mission is easy to file as a report and hard to understand in a minute. Without a designed journey, a visitor never sees why manual scheduling fails inside a pass, what the AI router does, or that the ground station is actually deciding.
  • Scope is the owned website only: Home, Mission, Architecture, Program, and Ground Station. Boundaries: no paid ads and no social campaign in this lab. The site is a demonstration — orbit time, link budget and battery are compressed, and it is not a live spacecraft feed.

3 Objectives

  • Position SomaiyaSat & SomaiyaPod with one offer: a PocketQube that deploys itself, decides what to send home, and serves amateur radio across four multiplexed modes.
  • Move a visitor from awareness to action with a primary call to action, “Open ground station”, and a secondary path, “Read the mission”.
  • Make the technical proof marketable: every routing choice is scored and explained, so the demo is also the trust message for faculty and for the HAM community named as the beneficiary.

4 Literature review

Strategy used to read the work: inbound content marketing, planned with the RACE journey (Reach, Act, Convert, Engage).

Chaffey & Ellis-Chadwick (2019) — owned media and a planned journey outperform one-off promotion when the goal is understanding, not a single transaction.

Pulizzi (2014) and Hollebeek & Macky (2019) — content earns trust when it answers the audience’s question; engagement follows usefulness, not louder claims.

Kotler, Kartajaya & Setiawan (2017) — Marketing 4.0 treats aware-to-advocate as a connected path. Isolated pages do not make a journey.

Gap. Student satellite projects usually stop at a technical report. They rarely publish a public path that both explains the constraint and lets a visitor watch the scheduler decide.

6 Implementation details

  • Page design. A dark mission-control identity separates the site from a generic college brochure. Headlines stay short, stats are scannable, and one amber action carries the eye. The nav never disappears, so no page is a dead end. Architecture shows SomaiyaPod → SomaiyaSat → ground segment as a block diagram a non-specialist can follow.
  • Strategy selected. Pull marketing through owned content, not paid reach. Audiences on the site itself: students and faculty (KJSIT / KJSSE), collaborator ReOrbit, and the global HAM community as beneficiary.
  • Tied to the product. The router scores each queued item as 0.40·priority + 0.25·urgency + 0.25·link margin + 0.10·power efficiency. Modes below their SNR floor are dropped first. Guard rails run before the policy: no usable link, a 25 s operator override, safe mode below 25% state of charge, and a watchdog if housekeeping is missed. The dashboard prints the rationale — the claim the marketing copy makes, shown rather than asserted.

7 Results & discussion — live pages from the deployed site

8 Key findings

  • One primary button plus a persistent nav joins five pages. Extra menus would compete with the demonstration.
  • The live stream is the engagement asset. Decisions are computed every simulated second, so the ground station can show the link floor, safe mode, and policy.
  • Explainable scores turn a power and bandwidth constraint into a trust message for faculty and for the amateur-radio audience.

9 Conclusion

The deployed site markets KJS-SRS-01 as a public, explainable mission. A visitor can meet the offer in one click or walk the longer story — problem, architecture, program, then proof. Students, faculty and the HAM community can see both the constraint (minutes of visibility, four modes, sub-1 W) and the response (autonomous routing with guard rails) without a briefing.

10 Future scope

  • Measure the journey: page views, CTA clicks, and time on the ground station.
  • Extend reach with Somaiya social channels, this poster’s QR code, and a one-page brief for amateur-radio clubs.
  • Still open: paid reach, a non-compressed link model, and audience testing by batch. The site is a demonstration, not a spacecraft feed.
